# Two-minute pitch

*(Demo open on the map, replay at the latest date. About 290 words.)*

**The gap.** Finland's cancer strategy wants every patient treated within target times, wherever they live. Its monitoring is an annual review of registry data that arrives about a year late. We asked: would a county notice sooner with a twin of its own cancer pathway?

**What we built.** Each wellbeing services county gets an aggregate twin, never a patient, just public THL and Cancer Registry numbers, re-estimated every month, with an interval on every number. *(point at map)* Red is a gap we can identify. Hatched means too few patients to judge, and we never call that fine.

**Where it sits.** *(click Päijät-Häme)* Here the gap is at the handover from primary to specialist care: 61% of people waiting for a first specialist assessment have waited over ninety days. Nationally it is 17%.

**The replay.** *(press play on the referral tab)* We replay 2021 to 2026. Both models get the same three pilot slots of the roadmap's fast-track measure. Model 3 decides once a year, as the strategy plans. Model 4 re-targets monthly from the twin. On referral, Model 4 reaches 51% of gap-months, Model 3 20%.

**Where we lost.** On the oncology queue it is a tie, 60 against 56: those gaps are chronic, and an annual review finds them too. The twin missed the 2020 pandemic dip. Survival and mortality we do not estimate, because no public county data supports it.

**The action.** *(county card)* For Päijät-Häme: standardised referral criteria and a symptom-based fast track, roadmap measure 2.5.2, owner named, Danish evidence of 17 days cited, effect in Finland not yet estimated, checked monthly, with the way it could fail written down.

**Monday.** One county, its own waiting-time data, and this runs on real intervals instead of proxies.
